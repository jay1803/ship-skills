# Decision methods for a proposal

Use only methods that clarify the project's actual decision.

- Compare the recommendation with the next-best use of the same resources.
  Make important opportunity costs visible rather than evaluating in isolation.
- For a material assumption, state evidence, uncertainty and what observation
  would change the recommendation. A cheap test can become the first milestone;
  proposing it does not authorize experiments or participant outreach.
- Use ranges or confidence estimates only when they have an explained basis.
  Separate known metric inputs from estimates in any driver model. Do not
  require a percentage for every claim or fabricate a numeric stopping rule.
- A premortem can expose a likely failure, its early signal and mitigation.
  Where useful, suggest a go/pivot/stop checkpoint; distinguish suggested
  criteria from the approver's accepted criteria.
- Assess the strongest reasonable alternative and evidence against the plan.
  More research is justified by a decision it could change, not completeness.
- At closeout, compare outcomes with the beliefs recorded at decision time.
  Distinguish a weak original decision from a reasonable decision followed by
  an adverse outcome. Do not rewrite the original expectations retroactively.
