# Project Proposal

Produce a decision-ready argument for leadership or stakeholders. Read the
[document contract](../project/references/document-contract.md) and use the
[proposal template](assets/proposal-template.md) when creating a new document;
retain a useful existing structure when refining one.

## Shape the decision

Identify the audience, decision requested and constraints from supplied notes,
research and existing drafts. Reuse them before asking questions. For a vague
idea, ask the few questions that determine the problem, intended outcome or
ask, while drafting what is known. If the user asks to draft with gaps, do so
without requiring an interview. Do not force a capture/refine pause.

Organize the argument around problem and evidence, alternatives (including
continuing as-is), recommendation, expected value, resources and tradeoffs,
risks, then the specific decision requested. Explain why now when supported;
do not invent urgency. A project may be operational, organizational or
cross-functional; business model, market and moat sections are conditional.

Keep scope and non-goals clear enough to judge the ask. Tie resources and
milestones to the next meaningful result. Distinguish a requested deadline
from a feasible plan and an allocated team from a suggested staffing model.
Success measures should identify baseline, target, observation window and
source where known; unknowns stay visible. Avoid false precision in forecasts.

For a short go/no-go brief, compress the same reasoning: commitment and
opportunity cost, payoff, cost, approach, critical assumptions, risks and a
Go / No-go / Not yet recommendation. This is advice, not recorded approval.
Use the [decision methods](references/decision-methods.md) when uncertainty
materially changes the recommendation; no numeric confidence ritual is needed.

## Review and handoff

When asked to prepare a submission-ready proposal, use
[proj-review](../proj-review/SKILL.md), address supported writing/evidence
findings within scope, and keep unresolved decisions explicit. A request for a
rough draft can end with a draft. Do not mark an unsupported claim resolved
just to satisfy review.

Save to the requested destination, reusing an existing proposal rather than
creating a competing plan. If none is named and a local artifact is requested,
use a descriptive `<project>-proposal.md` in the working directory. Record the
proposal revision, review state, open questions and next decision in its
continuation block. Return the draft or review-ready document and the exact
ask. Leave approval pending until actual evidence arrives; do not create the
Entry Page or claim kickoff merely because the proposal is finished.
